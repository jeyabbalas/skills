ch01-c001term

What is anecdotal evidence, and what are the four reasons the book gives for why it usually fails?

Answer

Evidence based on data that is unpublished and usually personal. It fails because of: small number of observations, selection bias, confirmation bias, and inaccuracy (misremembered, misrepresented, repeated inaccurately).

Good requires: the unpublished/personal definition plus at least three of the four failure modes, named. Easy: all four unprompted. Hard: definition right, failure modes vague.

ch01-c002term

Contrast a cross-sectional study with a longitudinal study, and say which the NSFG is.

Answer

Cross-sectional captures a snapshot of a group at a point in time; longitudinal observes the same group repeatedly over time. The NSFG is cross-sectional (repeated in cycles — we use Cycle 6, 2002–2003).

Good requires: both definitions, the contrast, and NSFG placed correctly. Easy: adds the cycle detail unprompted.

ch01-c003concept

The NSFG is not representative — it is deliberately oversampled. What does that mean, why did the designers do it, and what does it cost?

Answer

Three groups (Hispanics, African-Americans, teenagers) were recruited at rates higher than their population share, so each subgroup is large enough for valid inferences. The cost: statistics from the sample no longer transfer directly to the general population (the finalwgt weights exist to fix this).

Good requires: the mechanism, the why (subgroup power), and the cost. Hard: mechanism only.

ch01-c004term

What is a recode in the NSFG, with an example — and what is the book’s advice about using them?

Answer

A variable calculated from the raw data rather than collected directly — e.g. prglngth is wksgest when available, else mosgest × 4.33. Advice: prefer recodes when available, since they often encode consistency checks, unless you have a compelling reason to process raw data yourself.

Good requires: definition + the prglngth example (or another real one) + the advice. Hard: definition only.

ch01-c005apply

In CleanFemPreg, birthwgt_lb values 97, 98, 99 are replaced with np.nan. What do those codes mean, and what concretely goes wrong if you skip this step?

Answer

They are special codes: 97 NOT ASCERTAINED, 98 REFUSED, 99 DON’T KNOW. Left in place they behave as real weights and poison computations — the book’s example is a bogus 99-pound baby in any mean or histogram. As nan they propagate safely (nan arithmetic yields nan; pandas functions handle nan).

Good requires: what the codes are + a concrete bogus-result consequence + why nan is safe. Easy: quotes the propagation rule.

ch01-c006analyze

Adding a new column: df['totalwgt_lb'] = … versus df.totalwgt_lb = …. What does each actually do, and why is the second one wrong?

Answer

Dictionary syntax creates a real column in the DataFrame. Dot notation instead attaches a plain Python attribute to the DataFrame object — it is not treated as a column, so later column operations silently miss it.

Good requires: the attribute-not-column mechanism, not just “use brackets”. Hard: knows the rule but not the mechanism.

ch01-c007apply

Describe the book’s validation move for a freshly imported NSFG variable, and how it caught the 51-pound baby.

Answer

Compare value_counts() against the codebook’s published table per variable. For outcome the counts matched exactly; for birthwgt_lb the binned counts matched but the full listing exposed an impossible value (51 lb), fixed with df.loc[df.birthwgt_lb > 20, 'birthwgt_lb'] = np.nan.

Good requires: the compare-to-codebook move + the loc-based fix. Easy: writes the loc line unprompted.

ch01-c008concept

The book says to work on two levels at once. What are they, and what does the caseid 10229 example add?

Answer

The level of statistics and the level of context. Respondent 10229’s outcomes read [4 4 4 4 4 4 1] — statistically unremarkable, but in context the story of six miscarriages before a live birth; the data deserve empathy and the respondents respect.

Good requires: both levels + what the example shows about reading records as people.
