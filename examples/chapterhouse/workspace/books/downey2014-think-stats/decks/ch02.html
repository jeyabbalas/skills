ch02-c001term

Define distribution and histogram as the book does, and give the one-line Python idiom for computing frequencies with a dictionary.

Answer

The distribution of a variable is the values that appear and how many times each appears; a histogram is a mapping from values to frequencies (and the graph of it). Idiom: hist[x] = hist.get(x, 0) + 1 in a loop (or collections.Counter).

Good requires: both definitions + one working idiom. Easy: names Counter too.

ch02-c002concept

Two Hist behaviors worth knowing cold: what happens to nan values, and what does Freq return for a value never seen?

Answer

When a pandas Series is passed, nan values are dropped before counting; Freq of an absent value returns 0 (no KeyError).

The histogram of birthwgt_oz should in theory be uniform. What does it actually show, and what is the book’s explanation?

0 ounces is more common than other values, and 1 and 15 are less common — respondents round off birth weights near whole pounds. A measurement/reporting artifact, not biology.

ch02-c005term

The book distinguishes “mean” from “average”. State the distinction.

Answer

The mean is the specific summary statistic Σxᵢ/n; an “average” is any of several summary statistics you might choose to describe central tendency.

Good requires: mean as the formula-defined statistic, average as the family.

ch02-c006concept

Apples versus pumpkins: what is the example doing, and when does a mean stop being a good description?

Answer

Six half-pound apples are summarized well by their mean; three 1-lb, two 3-lb, and one 591-lb pumpkin have mean 100 lb — misleading because there is no typical pumpkin. A mean describes a sample well only when values cluster around it; with high spread or extreme values there may be no meaningful single-number summary.

Good requires: the point of the contrast, stated generally (not just retelling the pumpkins).

Compute Cohen’s d for first-baby vs other pregnancy lengths. At completion stage: present d = (x̄₁ − x̄₂)/s with the pooled-variance step blanked; the student supplies it and interprets the book’s value.

Answer

Pooled variance = (n₁s₁² + n₂s₂²)/(n₁ + n₂); s is its square root. The book’s result: the means differ by 0.029 standard deviations — tiny (the male–female height gap is ≈1.7 sd, for scale).

ch02-c009evaluate

You have the first-babies results: difference 0.078 weeks (13 hours), d = 0.029. Which summaries would get a story on the evening news, which would reassure an anxious patient — and what does the book say keeps either report honest?

Answer

News: the raw difference dramatized (13 hours! or “first babies arrive later”). Patient: the effect size in context (0.029 sd, ~0.2% of a pregnancy) or a probability framing relevant to her. Honesty: persuasion is fine, but reports must acknowledge uncertainty and limitations — the choice of statistic is itself a rhetorical act guided by professional ethics.

Good requires: audience-appropriate statistic for both cases + the acknowledge-uncertainty principle.
