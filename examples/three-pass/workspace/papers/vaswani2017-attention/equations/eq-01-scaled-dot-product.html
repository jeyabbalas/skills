Attention Is All You Need — Equation (1): Scaled Dot-Product Attention

Equation (1) · §3.2.1 · p.4 of paper.pdf

\[ \mathrm{Attention}(\htmlClass{term t1 term-q}{Q}, \htmlClass{term t2 term-k}{K}, \htmlClass{term t3 term-v}{V}) = \htmlClass{term t4 term-softmax}{\mathrm{softmax}}\left(\frac{\htmlClass{term t1 term-q}{Q}\htmlClass{term t2 term-k}{K^{\top}}}{\htmlClass{term t5 term-scale}{\sqrt{d_k}}}\right)\htmlClass{term t3 term-v}{V} \]

What it says

Every query is scored against every key by dot product; the scores are divided by \(\sqrt{d_k}\); a softmax turns each query’s scores into weights; the output for each query is the weight-averaged sum of the values. The matrix form processes “a set of queries simultaneously” (§3.2.1, p.4): with \(n\) queries and \(m\) key–value pairs, \(QK^{\top}\) is the \(n \times m\) score table, and the result is one \(d_v\)-dimensional output row per query.

Intuition

A learned kernel smoother. For each position, prediction = weighted average of other positions’ values, weights = normalized similarity between this position’s query and the others’ keys. Nothing in the weights is a fitted constant — they are recomputed from the data every time; what training fits are the projections that produce \(Q\), \(K\), \(V\) (§3.2.2, p.5).

Term by term

\(Q\) — the queries, “packed together into a matrix Q”; queries and keys have dimension \(d_k\) (§3.2.1, p.4). One row per position that is asking for information — the profile it wants matched.
\(K^{\top}\) — the keys, packed into matrix \(K\); \(QK^{\top}\) computes “the dot products of the query with all keys” (§3.2.1, p.4). What every position advertises about itself; a big dot product with a query means “relevant to that query.”
\(V\) — the values, packed into matrix \(V\), of dimension \(d_v\); the output is “a weighted sum of the values” (§3.2, p.3–4). The content that actually gets averaged — keys decide how much, values decide of what.
\(\mathrm{softmax}\) — applied to the scaled scores “to obtain the weights on the values” (§3.2.1, p.4). The multinomial-logit link: real-valued scores in, positive weights that sum to 1 out — turning scores into an averaging scheme.
\(\sqrt{d_k}\) — “divide each by \(\sqrt{d_k}\)” (§3.2.1, p.4); footnote 4: with independent mean-0, variance-1 components, \(q \cdot k\) has mean 0 and variance \(d_k\) (p.4). A z-score: the raw dot product’s spread grows like \(\sqrt{d_k}\), so dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) keeps scores at unit variance no matter how wide the keys are.

Where it’s used

  • Inside every head of multi-head attention: \(\mathrm{head}_i = \mathrm{Attention}(QW_i^Q, KW_i^K, VW_i^V)\) (§3.2.2, p.5).
  • All three attention sites of the model — encoder-decoder attention, encoder self-attention, decoder self-attention (§3.2.3, p.5).
  • The decoder’s masking lives inside this equation: illegal connections are set to \(-\infty\) “in the input of the softmax” so their weights vanish (§3.2.3, p.5).

Dot-product (multiplicative) attention predates this paper [2, 3 cited §3.2.1, p.4]; the contribution named here is the \(1/\sqrt{d_k}\) scaling. The variance argument for it is a footnote-level heuristic, not a theorem — the reader has pinned verifying it by hand: margin note N0002; the weights-vs-coefficients bridge is N0001.
